# Target Users + Problems 

The tagret users have previously been introduced in the value proposition. 
I have made small adjustments to them based on our recent discoveries.

**Persona A — The student living in the dorm** 

**Julia Meier, 21 years old, 3rd semester, Business Administration**

Julia moved into her dorm half a year ago and while she has tried cooking in the shared kitchen it is not as clean as it could be if everyone pulled their weight. People leave dirty dishes in the sink, surfaces are sticky and the oven looks like it needs a deep cleaning. Furthermore she doesn’t see the point cooking when it’s only for herself. She buys a bag of spinach, uses a handful, and watches the rest go soggy in the fridge. She’s tried meal prepping once. It lasted four days before she gave up and ordered pizza. If not returning home with a bag of frozen chicken wing for dinner she ends up scrolling through Wolt or UberEats, feeling vaguely guilty about spending 14€ on a burger that arrives lukewarm in a pile of plastic. She knows it’s not sustainable, but cooking a full meal at 9pm after a long day just feels like too much. So she doesn’t.

What Julia actually wants is simple: something warm, real, and cheap, without having to think too hard about it. She doesn’t need a gourmet experience.

Dorm & Dine is exactly that for her. She opens the app, sees what’s available two floors up, taps reserve, and shows up at the right time. No account needed just to browse. No delivery fee. No guilt.

**Persona B — The hobby cook living in the same dorm**

**Lars Müller, 23 years old, 5th semester, Computer Science**

Lars actually likes cooking. His shared kitchen is not exactly busy. Not many others cook. Yet they use the kitchen dirty. There is always dishes in the sink, He needs to sweep surfaces before he can get to prepping ingredients and using the oven leaves him annoyed and disgusted. He wishes people would clean up after themselves more. But he wont let others get in the way of his passion: He enjoys making food like curries, stir-fries, the occasional homemade soup and he always makes too much, because cooking for one portion feels almost pointless. The extra food usually ends up in a container in his fridge, eaten reluctantly over the next two days, or quietly thrown away when it’s been there too long. He’s not struggling financially, but as a student he notices every euro. The idea that his grocery bill could partly pay for itself or that someone might help with the dishes afterwards is genuinely appealing.

Lars is a little worried about interacting with others. The dorm isnt exactly neighboorly. Everyone does their own thing. With Dorm & Dine, Lars posts what he’s cooking that evening, sets a price that covers his ingredients, and gets a knock on the door at pickup time.
