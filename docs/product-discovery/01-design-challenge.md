# Design Challenge

## Final Design Challenge

Important for this (or any) section was to filter for surveyors who fit our user profile and live in a Wohnheim. 
The question about their living situation was added as a way for us to learn if people living alone or with family could potentially be interested in this project and what it aims to create, and therefore increase our target user range. 
This is still an open design decision at the moment. 
The majority of people living in a Wohnheim seem to have in common that they like to cook yet they believe the dorm kitchens are too dirty. 
This might be the driving cause for 3/ 5 of those living in student dorms to order takeout more often. 

“If someone in your area offered a fresh, home-cooked meal for 2€–4€, would you buy it?“ was by far one of the more important questions for us to ask as the responses confirmed an interest in what we are trying to build. 
3 people were neutral about it, while 5 were leaning more towards a positive approach. 
On the other hand the question about willingness to sell meals showed us a potential hurdle in our path: While 3 people are interested in selling food, 5 are leaning towards only being buyers (if anything). 
Which could suggest we’d have more demand than supply.  

People are most worried about 2 things. 1) Cleaning up after cooking. And 2) Managing buyers. 
Overall there is not a big interest in the social aspect of the service idea. 
Out of 9 responses not a single person thought it would be a natural way to connect with neighbors. 
For 6 people the primary focus is saving money, while 3 people genuinely just want to receive/ hand off their food and go about their day. 
A majority of people (7) are in some shape or form interested in a payment method for food that involves a service. 
The example provided in the form was “cleaning the dishes” yet only 2 people said they are willing to do that.

While we initially thought a rating system could be really discouraging for cooks, even the people willing to cook are interested in both a feature to see how many meals a cook sold, and a feature that lets buyers rate the cook. 

The last question was about security. 5 people want only students to have access to this type of app, while 3 don't care about that at all. Only 1 person thought standard email login is fine. This would mean our idea to expand our target group would only include students. 

The next logical questions we have to ask ourselves are: 
**How might we provide students living in dorms a more cost efficient and healthy alternative, when preparing meals in their kitchen is made more difficult because of health/ cleanliness reasons?**

**How might we encourage people to cook in their dorm kitchen and make sure the shared space is clean before and after to create a market for dishes/ meals?**

**How might we create an environment of trust inside the dorm so that neighbors are more willing to buy from each other?** 

**How might we implement a way for buyers to pay for a meal with a service and what would those services include? Are they pre-determined or can they make an offer to a cook and the cook decides if they want to receive the service in exchange for the meal?**

**How might we implement a fair rating system that is not discouraging the cooks?**
